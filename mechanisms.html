Mechanisms

From trait to behavior

The scale answers how much. It still has to answer why and how. Two people with Possession 7 may be living opposite things, and each one's partner sees different behaviors.

The chain

root → property → expression trait → behavior

The root is where the value comes from (attachment, trauma, culture, belief, scarcity, projection). The expression trait is the personality filter that decides the form (aggressive or peaceful). The behavior is what the other sees. It is the only thing the other sees.

Equifinality

Different roots, same value

Possession 7 from fear and Possession 7 from feeling like an owner score the same on the scale.

Multifinality

Same root, different behaviors

The same fear of loss becomes a scene in an aggressive person and silent clinging in a peaceful one.

Cross-cutting roots

The moment and neediness

A closed person marks everything low for everyone; a needy person marks everything high. Neither is their pattern.

The signature of the root

The root leaves three marks the scale captures without asking why do you feel this.

1

The accompanying emotion

Anger, fear, shame and resentment are different roots speaking.

2

The trigger

What has to happen for the value to rise.

3

What the person wants

Obedience, reassurance, being chosen, more time, proof.

Model case: Possession and jealousy

Does jealousy come from the idea of property or from the fear of losing and sharing? From both, and from five more roots, and each one produces a different jealousy. Jealousy is one name for at least seven mechanisms.

Anger points to property; fear to loss; shame to comparison; resentment to scarcity. Property fires without a rival: the other's autonomy is enough. Fear of loss needs a threat, even an imagined one. Comparison needs a concrete rival. Scarcity needs divided time. What the person asks for after the crisis gives away the root: a rule, reassurance, praise, time, proof.

Jealousy is not envy

Jealousy is triangular (me, them, a third party) and fears losing what it has. Envy is a pair (me and whoever has what I want).

The historical root of property is literal

Marriage as a contract of ownership, the wife as transferred goods, adultery as a crime against the husband. Jealousy by property is the residue of that model speaking in the first person.

Compersion

Feeling joy at a partner's joy with someone else is the inverse of jealousy, and shows that the Possession value can approach zero by choice and practice.

The expression trait: aggressiveness and peacefulness

The trait doesn't change the value; it changes what the other sees. It is what most confuses the Couple Test: the peaceful person is read as not jealous and the aggressive one as too jealous, when both may have the same 7.

Peacefulness is not absence

It is the same value without noise. We never fight with Openness 2 is not peace; it is a closed channel.

Both extremes have their own shadow

The aggressive tends toward control and violence; the peaceful toward passive aggression, accumulated resentment and late explosions. The healthy point has a name: assertiveness.

The roots of each property

Attraction

  • Real attraction, of body and chemistry
  • Novelty (the person is new, and novelty pulls)
  • Status they lend me
  • Prohibition (what I can't have attracts me more)
  • Resemblance to someone from the past

Passion

  • Attraction plus novelty
  • Limerence (a void of mine filled by the person)
  • Intermittence (the person vanishes and returns, and that is addictive)
  • Prohibition
  • Neediness (hunger for a bond pointed at someone)

Admiration

  • Real merit
  • Idealization (Passion disguised as admiration)
  • Status the person lends me
  • Compensation (I admire in them what I lack)
  • Reputation (what people say, before I saw for myself)

Affection

  • Real, built affection
  • Habit and familiarity (the comfort of the known)
  • Pity (downward compassion)
  • Gratitude
  • Duty (I care because I must)

Intimacy

  • Years of living together
  • A crisis gone through together (accelerated intimacy)
  • Identification (the person is like me)
  • Imagined intimacy (I know them by what they show, not by what they are)

Openness

  • A genuinely open channel
  • Avoidant attachment (I close by default)
  • Shame
  • I was punished before for opening up
  • Culture (men don't talk)
  • Accumulated contempt that closed the channel

Compatibility

  • Real fit
  • Similarity (we are alike)
  • Complementarity (we are different and it fits)
  • Adaptation (one of us molded to the other)
  • Denial (we pretend it fits)

Trust

  • Their actual behavior
  • Past betrayal by someone else (I distrust in advance)
  • Generalized distrust (I distrust everyone)
  • Projection of my own disloyalty

Support

  • Affection
  • Duty
  • Bargaining chip (I give to collect later)
  • Guilt
  • Fear of being dispensable

Protection

  • Real care
  • Property (I defend what's mine)
  • Projection of my own vulnerability
  • Need to be needed
  • Control disguised as watchfulness

Investment

  • Love
  • Sunk cost (I've invested too much to leave)
  • Guilt and duty
  • Fear of being alone
  • Pride in not giving up

Commitment

  • Decision
  • Inertia and habit
  • Obligation
  • Lack of alternatives
  • Sunk cost
  • Fear of the judgment of those who will find out

Possession

  • Property (they're mine)
  • Fear of loss
  • Comparison (low self-worth)
  • Scarcity (fear of sharing time and attention)
  • Betrayal trauma
  • Projection (my own wish to stray)
  • Cultural script (jealousy proves love)

Dependence

  • Neediness (hunger to be cared for)
  • Anxious attachment
  • No network of one's own
  • Fused identity
  • Material dependence
  • Learned helplessness (I can't cope without them)

Freedom

  • Real freedom
  • Control by the other (their Possession)
  • My own guilt or duty (I trap myself)
  • Dependence (I can't leave, so I feel trapped)
  • Cultural script (that's what a relationship is)
  • Fear of being alone

Time

  • Real duration
  • Nostalgia (I love who the person was, not who they are)
  • Projection (I love who they will be)
  • Habit

Reciprocity

  • Real balance
  • Low self-esteem (they can't possibly like me)
  • Expectation too high
  • Bookkeeping (I count what I give)

Shadows

  • Unrepaired hurt
  • Rivalry (we compete)
  • Fear (of losing, of rejection, of intimacy)
  • Contempt
  • Boredom
  • Shame
