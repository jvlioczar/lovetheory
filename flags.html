The heuristics of love

Red flags and green flags

Nobody measures eighteen properties before deciding whether to let someone in. We use shortcuts: quick signals, learned through experience, culture and biology, that predict whether a person is a threat or tends to work out. Heuristics are the fast layer of the theory; the scale is the slow one. The two fail in different ways.

How a flag works

A flag is an observable signal (behavior, phrase, habit) that the brain converts into a value prediction for one or more properties, before having evidence.

Three kinds of flag

Character

The most reliable

They predict Trust, Admiration and Shadows. Character is what changes least.

Dynamic

Depend on the pair

They predict Possession, Dependence, Openness, Freedom and Reciprocity. Someone can be a red flag for me and a green flag for someone else.

Compatibility

Good for me, now

They predict Compatibility, Time and Commitment. They don't say whether the person is good; they say whether they are good for me at this moment.

Where heuristics fail

False red

Trauma paints neutral things red. Someone who was betrayed reads a late reply as infidelity. The flag is in the reader, not in the person.

False green

Charm, beauty, status and intensity are read as green flags and predict nothing about Trust or Commitment. The halo effect is the most expensive heuristic in love.

Beige flags

Signals that predict nothing but the reader treats as if they did: music taste, football team, star sign.

Cultural flags

What is red in one culture is green in another: affection in public, jealousy as proof of love, splitting the bill.

My flags

The flags you use say what you fear and what you seek. Someone with ten red flags about control and none about coldness is telling their own story.
