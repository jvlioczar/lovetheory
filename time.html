Time

What time does

Time is not only a property; it is a force acting on all the others, and it acts hardest on Passion. It is the part of love most people mistake for it's over.

What time does, on average

Passion falls.

The intense passion phase lasts 12 to 30 months in most studies. The brain habituates to the predictable; what fires dopamine is novelty.

Affection, Intimacy, Trust and Commitment rise,

if the bond survives the fall. It is the passage from passionate to companionate love.

The wish to be together is a sum:

Passion, Affection (comfort) and Compatibility of daily life. Monotony erodes all three in different ways: it takes the novelty out of Passion, turns comfort into boredom and reduces daily life to logistics: bills, children, schedules.

Possession and Dependence can rise

out of habit even while Passion falls: that is staying together by inertia.

What sustains or rekindles

Shared novelty.

Doing new things together. Couples who do new and exciting activities together report more satisfaction and more attraction than those who do pleasant but familiar ones (Aron, 2000). The ingredient is novelty, not pleasure.

Creativity and play.

Humor, games, surprise. Inventing rituals instead of only repeating the existing ones.

Diversifying.

Places, topics, people, roles. Learning something together, traveling, changing the scenery, swapping who decides.

Autonomy that feeds desire.

Desire needs distance, mystery and seeing the other in their own world, shining away from you (Perel). Total fusion kills Passion; separate lives that meet keep it alive.

Rituals of reconnection.

Small bids for attention answered, daily rituals, conversation that is not logistics (Gottman).

Intimate novelty.

Sexual curiosity, repertoire, talking about desire.

What does not work:

intermittence. Disappearing and coming back rekindles Passion through anxiety, not desire; it is addictive and erodes Trust. It is the toxic version of novelty.
