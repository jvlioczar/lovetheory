Glossary

The site's terms, and the ones outside it

Everything the site names, plus the terms you will find elsewhere about the same subject. Each with one line and the way to the page where it lives.

The model

Property
What one feels for someone: a dimension of the bond, like Trust or Possession. The site has eighteen. see →
Sub-property
A nameable component of a property. Trust has honesty, loyalty, competence, predictability, emotional and physical safety. see →
Item
The concrete question that measures a sub-property on the scale. see →
Value
The measure of what is felt, from 0 to 10, at that moment. Zero is a legitimate value. see →
Weight
How much a property matters to you, separate from how much of it you feel. The difference between value and weight is one of the most revealing results. see →
Variability
How much a value or a position swings over time. The word is variability, not inconsistency: inconsistency judges, variability describes. see →
Profile
The set of one person's eighteen values for another. see →
Portrait
A dated profile. The test always produces a portrait, never a truth; that's why it's worth redoing. see →
Love type
A region of the portrait space: a recognizable pattern of high and low properties, like philia or storge. Never a goal. see →
Modulator
What, in the one who feels, distorts the value: attachment, personality, trauma, moment, neediness, gender role. see →
Shadow
A negative-sign property that is also part of the bond: hurt, rivalry, fear, contempt. On the radar, the gray layer. see →
Root
Where a value comes from. The same value can have different roots: Possession 7 from fear of loss or from feeling like an owner. see →
Expression trait
How a value shows on the outside. The same value comes out differently in an aggressive and in a peaceful person. see →
Mechanism
The whole chain: root, property, expression trait, behavior. Only the behavior is what the other sees. see →
Heuristic
The shortcut we use to predict whether it will work out: red flags and green flags. see →
Constellation
The overlay of three or more people's portraits: what repeats among those you keep close is a portrait of you. see →
Cut-off line
A Compatibility sub-property that is binary, not a scale: children, religion, where to live, what one eats. see →
Availability thermometer
Five items before the scale that measure how open or closed to connection the person is right now. It doesn't alter the values; it alters the reading. see →
Self map
The result of Self Discover: where you are on each slider, and where society presumes you. see →
Presumed (the left-hand rule)
On every slider, the leftmost position is what society presumes of someone until proven otherwise. Not the right one nor the common one: the presumed one. see →
Equifinality
Different roots reach the same value. Possession 7 from fear and Possession 7 from property score the same. see →
Multifinality
The same root produces different behaviors. The same fear of loss becomes a scene in an aggressive person and silent clinging in a peaceful one. see →
Cross-cutting root
A root that distorts all values at once: the moment (closed to connection) and neediness (everything high). see →
Sources of value
Where the image you form of someone comes from: direct perception, expectation, positive and negative discoveries, reputation, approval of the circle. see →
Expectation (expected value)
What you expected to feel or find before you knew the person. High expectation is the raw material of idealization. see →
Discovery
What raised or lowered the value over time. The distance between expected and felt is the story of the bond told in discoveries. see →
Reputation
What people say about the person. Weighs on Trust and Admiration before any evidence; the biggest source of borrowed flags. see →
Approval of the circle
What friends and family say about the bond. Predicts duration better than common sense admits; the Romeo and Juliet effect did not hold up. see →

The eighteen properties

Attraction
What pulls me toward the person: physical, charm, sexual, fetish, intellectual, chemistry, status, aesthetic. see →
Passion
How much the person occupies my mental space: frequency of thought, euphoria, idealization, urgency, fascination, obsession. see →
Admiration
What elevates me in the person: character, talent, achievements, style, courage, respect, pride, inspiration. You can love without admiring. see →
Affection
The warmth: tenderness, care, missing them, comfort in their presence, non-sexual touch, compassion. see →
Intimacy
How well we know each other: the stock of what is already known. Different from Openness, which is the channel open now. see →
Openness
The channel between us, in both directions: outbound (emotional openness, what I show) and inbound (receptivity, what I take in). see →
Emotional openness
The outbound side of the channel: how much of what I feel I say and show, including what I hide. see →
Emotional receptivity
The inbound side of the channel: how willing I am to receive what the other feels, without defending or minimizing. see →
Compatibility
How well our lives fit: values, practical life, intimate, daily life, project. Fit, not admiration. see →
Trust
How safe I feel: honesty, loyalty, competence, predictability, emotional and physical safety. see →
Support
What they do for me and I for them: emotional, practical, material, availability. Two directions, I give and I receive. see →
Protection
I place myself between them and what threatens them. The shadow is overprotection, which turns into control. see →
Investment
What I spend on them: time, money, energy, priority, opportunity cost, sacrifice. see →
Commitment
The decision to stay: intention, plans, agreed exclusivity, resistance to alternatives, duty, habit. see →
Possession
How much I want them to be mine: jealousy, desired exclusivity, control, fear of loss, comparison, territoriality. see →
Dependence
How much I need them: emotional, practical, social, identity, fusion. Possession is I want to have you; dependence is I can't manage without you. see →
Freedom
How much I remain myself inside the bond. The received side of the other's Possession; low, it is feeling trapped. see →
Time
The shape of the bond over time: duration, frequency, rhythm, phase, projection, nostalgia, novelty, monotony. see →
Reciprocity
What I think comes back: perception of being loved, balance of effort, gratitude, debt, known asymmetry. see →
Shadows
What makes up the bond with a negative sign: hurt, rivalry, fear, anger, contempt, boredom, pity, shame. see →
Novelty and monotony
How much is still new between us, and the routine that erases. It is what time does to Passion. see →

Mechanisms and modulators

Attachment
The way someone bonds and reacts to distance and loss. A trait of the one who feels, not a value of the bond. see →
Secure attachment
Trusts, asks, lets go, comes back. Reads values closest to real. see →
Anxious attachment
Fears abandonment; raises Possession, Dependence and Passion; marks threat where there is none. see →
Avoidant attachment
Closes by default; lowers Openness, Intimacy and declared Dependence. see →
Disorganized attachment
Wants and fears at the same time; swings between anxious and avoidant. Common after abuse. see →
Big Five
The five major personality traits: openness to experience, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism. see →
Neuroticism
The tendency to intense, swinging negative emotions. On the site, the Emotional reactivity slider. see →
Neediness
Hunger for a bond: not lack of contact, lack of being cared for. Inflates Dependence, Passion, Investment and Possession; lowers standards. see →
Loneliness
Lack of contact. Not neediness: you can be surrounded by people and needy, or alone and whole. see →
Availability for connection
The state, not the trait: how open or closed the person is right now to letting someone in. see →
Window of tolerance
The range in which a person can feel without shutting down or exploding (Siegel). Outside it, values come out distorted. see →
Gender role
The script society assigns to each gender within the bond: who provides, cares, initiates, yields, protects, shows emotion. see →
Aggressiveness and peacefulness
The main expression trait: the aggressive confronts, the peaceful goes quiet. Neither changes the value. see →
Assertiveness
The healthy point between the two: I say what I feel, without attacking and without going silent. see →
Property (root)
Jealousy from feeling like an owner: fires at the other's autonomy, no rival needed; asks for obedience. see →
Fear of loss
Jealousy from anticipating abandonment: fires at absence and any third party; asks for reassurance. see →
Comparison
Jealousy from low self-worth: needs a concrete rival; asks to be chosen. see →
Scarcity
Jealousy from fear of sharing time and attention: fires at friends, work, screens; asks for priority. see →
Betrayal trauma
Hypervigilance to the exact trigger of an old betrayal, lived with someone else. Today's person pays for another. see →
Projection
Attributing one's own impulse to the other: accusing them of straying because you want to stray. see →
Cultural script
What culture teaches us to feel and to perform: jealousy as proof of love, for instance. see →
Jealousy and envy
Jealousy is triangular (me, them, a third party) and fears losing what it has. Envy is a pair (me and whoever has what I want). see →
Compersion
Feeling joy at a partner's joy with someone else. The inverse of jealousy. see →
Sunk cost
Continuing because you've invested too much to leave (Rusbult). The most common reason to stay and the one least tied to love. see →
Idealization
Seeing more than there is. Passion disguised as admiration; high expectation is the raw material. see →
Limerence
The state of obsession with someone, with euphoria, withdrawal and idealization (Tennov). The shadow of Passion; lasts 12 to 30 months. see →
Intermittence
Vanishing and coming back. Rekindles Passion through anxiety, not desire; addictive and erodes Trust. The toxic version of novelty. see →
Intermittent reinforcement
The mechanism behind intermittence: unpredictable reward holds more than constant reward. see →
Hedonic adaptation
The brain habituates to the predictable. That's why Passion falls. see →
Self-expansion
Doing new things together sustains attraction (Aron). The ingredient is novelty, not pleasure. see →
The four horsemen
Criticism, contempt, defensiveness and stonewalling: the four patterns that, for Gottman, best predict the end. see →
Stonewalling
Shutting down and no longer responding during conflict. The peaceful expression of anger. see →
Bids for attention
The small gestures that ask for a response through the day. Answering them is what, for Gottman, keeps the bond. see →
Halo effect
Reading charm, beauty, status and intensity as signs of character. The most expensive false green in love. see →
Red flag, green flag, beige flag
A sign of threat, a sign it tends to work out, and a sign that predicts nothing but is treated as if it did. see →
False red
Trauma paints neutral things red. The flag is in the reader, not in the person. see →
Love bombing
Enormous intensity in the first week: gifts, declarations, presence. High Passion with baseless Trust. see →
Gaslighting
Making someone doubt their own memory and perception. A form of manipulation; a red line in Stay or Go. see →
Emotional blackmail
Using guilt, fear or threat to get what one wants. Manipulation. see →
Ghosting
Disappearing without explanation. Intermittence taken to the end. see →
Breadcrumbing
Giving crumbs of attention to keep someone close without commitment. Intermittent reinforcement on purpose. see →
Orbiting
Leaving someone's life but keeping watch from afar, usually through social media. see →
Benching
Keeping someone on the bench: neither moving forward nor letting go, to keep the option. see →
Trauma bonding
The bond that forms with someone who alternates abuse and affection. Explains why leaving is so hard. see →
Codependency
Needing to be needed: organizing one's life around caring for someone who doesn't care for themselves. Two-way dependence. see →
Social capital
What a person is worth in the eyes of others: prestige, circle, success, recognized beauty. And how much of it they lend to whoever is with them. see →
Status
A person's place on the social ladder. A sub-property of Attraction and a root of Admiration; it weighs on almost everyone's attraction, to some degree. see →
Hypergamy
The tendency to choose someone higher on the social ladder. Described as a human trait, not as anyone's flaw. see →
Social proof
Finding someone attractive because others do. The value comes from the queue, not the person. see →
Trophy partner
The person chosen in part for what they say about the one who chose them. High Bond in public with Admiration through pride in being seen. see →
Borrowed importance
Feeling important for being with someone important. Usually comes with dependence on approval in Self Love. see →

Types of love and structures

Eros
High Attraction and Passion. The love that pulls. see →
Philia
Friendship: high Compatibility, Trust and Openness, Attraction near zero. see →
Storge
Family love: Affection, Commitment and long time, often without admiration and without choice. see →
Agape
High Support and Affection without expecting a return. The love that gives. see →
Ludus
Attraction and light company, zero Commitment. Love as a game. see →
Pragma
Commitment, Investment and Compatibility with low Passion. The love that chooses and builds. see →
Mania
High Passion, Possession and Dependence. The love that consumes. see →
Philautia
Self-love. The same ruler pointed inward: Self Love. see →
Xenia
Hospitality: affection toward the stranger. see →
Triangular theory
Intimacy, passion and commitment (Sternberg) generate eight types. The Love Theory is a higher-resolution version: eighteen properties. see →
Consummate love
High Intimacy, Passion and Commitment at the same time. Rare and unstable; time moves one of the three. see →
Companionate love
High Intimacy, Affection, Trust and Commitment with low Passion. The love of those who stayed. see →
Infatuation
High Passion with no intimacy and no commitment. Limerence. see →
Empty love
High Commitment with the rest low. One stays by decision, habit or duty. see →
Parasocial love
Fan love: high Admiration, Passion and Investment, zero Reciprocity, imagined intimacy (Horton and Wohl). see →
Platonic
Intimacy and Commitment without romantic or sexual attraction. see →
Queerplatonic
A bond stronger and more committed than ordinary friendship, without being romantic. Born in the aromantic community. see →
Residual love
What remains of an ex: high time and nostalgia, zero Commitment, shadows in between. see →
Monogamy
Agreed and, in general, desired exclusivity. see →
Monogamish
Not quite traditional monogamy: tends toward closed relationships, with some agreed flexibility. The term is Dan Savage's. see →
Ethical non-monogamy
Any structure without exclusivity where everyone knows and agrees: open relationship, polyamory, relationship anarchy. see →
Open relationship
A couple that admits other bonds, usually sexual, with agreed rules. see →
Polyamory
More than one loving bond at the same time, with everyone's knowledge. see →
Relationship anarchy
No prior hierarchy between bonds: each relationship defines its own rules. see →
Metamour
Your partner's partner, with whom you have no direct bond. see →
Polycule
The network of people linked by polyamorous bonds. see →
NRE (new relationship energy)
The euphoria at the start of a bond, named by the polyamorous community. Passion before time acts. see →
Situationship
A bond with no name and no agreement: Commitment undefined by choice. The assumed layer tends to diverge. see →
Friends with benefits
Friendship with sex and no agreed romantic commitment. see →
Talking stage
The phase before the bond, when the two get to know each other with nothing agreed. see →
Companion animal love
Very high Affection, Support and Trust, zero Attraction, one-sided Openness. Full love by the model. see →

Sexuality and attraction

Sexual orientation
Which way sexual attraction points: hetero, homo, bi, pan, asexual and others. see →
Romantic orientation
Which way romantic attraction points, separate from the sexual one: aromantic, biromantic and others. see →
Split attraction model
The idea that sexual and romantic attraction are separate axes. Explains I love but don't desire and I desire but don't love. see →
Bisexual
Attraction to more than one gender. see →
Pansexual
Attraction in which gender is not a criterion. On the site, a marker apart from the slider, not a point on it. see →
Omnisexual
Attraction to all genders, with gender still noticed. Close to pan. see →
Asexual
Little or no sexual attraction. A spectrum, not a box. see →
Gray-A
The gray zone of the asexual spectrum: rare or weak sexual attraction. see →
Demisexual
Sexual attraction only after an emotional bond. see →
Aromantic
Little or no romantic attraction. Aromantic people love friends, family, animals. see →
Aroace
Aromantic and asexual. see →
Types of attraction
Sexual, romantic, sensual, aesthetic, platonic, intellectual. They are the sub-properties of the Attraction family. see →
Sapiosexual
Attraction above all to intelligence. The intellectual sub-property of Attraction as the main criterion. see →
Libido
How much sexual desire the person feels, in general. Varies with time, health and phase; not a defect. see →
Kink and fetish
Practices and preferences outside the common sexual script. Consensual, common, a sub-property of Attraction. see →
Vanilla
The common sexual script, without kink. The presumed standard of the sexual openness slider. see →
Gender identity
Which gender the person identifies with: cis, trans, non-binary, fluid. Not orientation and not expression. see →
Cisgender
Someone who identifies with the gender assigned at birth. see →
Transgender
Someone who identifies with a gender different from the one assigned at birth. see →
Non-binary
Someone who doesn't identify only as a man or only as a woman: outside the two, or between them. see →
Genderfluid
An identity that moves over time. On the site, the slider's variability, not a position. see →
Gender expression
How the person presents: clothes, voice, gesture. Separate from identity and role. see →
Kinsey scale
The 1948 scale from 0 (hetero) to 6 (homo). The site's orientation slider is that scale in five positions. see →
Fisher's three systems
Lust, romantic attraction and attachment as separate brain circuits, which can point to different people at the same time. see →
Love languages
Words, time, gifts, acts, touch (Chapman). Weak evidence as a theory; useful as a preference for giving and receiving. see →

Stay or Go and red lines

Red line
What does not enter the balance: violence, threats, coercion, betrayal, compulsive lying, manipulation, control, humiliation. Reasons in themselves. see →
Balance
Pros and cons, each with a score and a weight, as a percentage on each side. Never a verdict. see →
Score and weight
The score is what you feel; the weight is how much it matters to you. The balance recalculates when the weight changes. see →
Reasons of love
Love, affection and history, growing together. see →
Reasons of prison
Habit, fear of being alone, duty, money, sunk cost, hope of change, fear of what people will say, not knowing who I would be. see →
Reasons of responsibility
Children. Legitimate, and neither love nor prison. see →
Repair
What happens after the break: naming, acknowledging, changing. A single betrayal only resolves with real repair, accepted by both. see →
Sexual coercion
Pressuring, insisting after a no, treating sex as an obligation. A red line from the first time. see →
Coercive control
Controlling money, phone, clothes, whom one talks to; isolating from friends and family. A red line. see →
Manipulation
Making the other doubt themselves, emotional blackmail, guilt as a weapon. A red line. see →
